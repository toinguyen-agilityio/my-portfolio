I took the morning train from Da Nang with a bag and no schedule. The ride is only a few hours, but the line hugs the coast and every window is a postcard.

The train crawls over the Hải Vân pass, so there is time to watch the sea appear and disappear between tunnels. I stopped checking the clock somewhere around the second one.

## Eating my way through town

Huế is a city you walk with your stomach. I started each day with a bowl of bún bò and ended it with sweet soup by the river.

![A bowl of bún bò Huế on a red plastic stool](/images/story/travel/hue-food-and-trains/food-bun-bo.jpg)

Most places have a handful of plastic stools and one dish they have made for decades. Pointing and smiling worked better than any phrasebook.

## What I took home

Slow travel gives you time to notice details, the same way a good interface does.

I came back with fewer photos than usual and a better memory of each day. Nothing was optimised: no route, no checklist, just the next meal.

Next time I will book the same seat on the same train and leave the afternoon empty on purpose.
